# Receiver lookahead can be forced to be arbitrarily large at the same near-benchmark periodic density

Independent research draft. Write $L=\log N$, let $p=p_N=1-\sigma z_N/\sqrt N-BL/N$ as in r092, $\eta=1-p$, and $\log Q_N=\mu N-d_N+c_Q L+o(L)$. The unit periodic torus has Lebesgue mass one; density below always refers to the actual binary source. Like r096, record the coordinate order in which receiver coordinates become available. A physical box selection can depend on the entire receiver, but the same box is used for the entire independent sample inside its sides.

## 1. A compulsory information counterexample for every physical witness selection

Fix all sufficiently large $N$, put $n=N-1$, $k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, and use receiver coordinates $(y,z)\in\mathbb T^n\times\mathbb T$. There is a density $d_N^\circ$ satisfying $\log(1/d_N^\circ)=\log Q_N+o_B(L)$ and independent of the parameter $m$ below. For every integer $m\ge8$ there exists one finite periodic binary step set $E_m$ with
\[
 |E_m|=d_N^\circ,\qquad |\{M_S1_{E_m}>p_N\}|\ge 1-N^{-2}.             \tag{1}
\]
Every measurable selection of real boxes containing their actual receiver, deterministic or randomized before the box is sampled, with genuine strict average $>p$ on a fraction/probability $\theta$, must satisfy
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^k I(A_i;X_{>i}\mid X_{\le i},A_{<i})
 \ge I(A_{\le k};z\mid X_{\le k})
 \ge (\theta-Ce^{-cN})\log(m/7)-\log2 .                               \tag{2}
\]
Here $A_i$ is the actual $i$th side (or its actual real representative in the periodic extension), $X=(y,z)$ is uniform, $c,C>0$ are independent of $m$, and the last coordinate is the specified $z$. In particular at fixed $N$ in the theorem, $\theta\ge1/2$ forces the minimum information over *every* admissible successful selection to diverge with $m$, while the density is unchanged. One can also take $\theta=1-1/N$. This is not obtained by decorating a witness with irrelevant bits: a coarse statistic of its actual first $k$ side lengths identifies the last receiver panel up to an interval of measure $O(1/m)$. The counterexample concerns genuine macroscopic physical boxes and one common source. It does not give a score exceeding $Q_N$ by a power.

### A finite periodic base source at the original price

Take in dimension $n$ a bounded equal-weight r093 cross at large finite thickness, near its fixed-indicator price at $p+2L/N$. As in r095 §1 the logarithm of that price is $\log Q_N+o_B(L)$, including the change from $n$ to $N$. It can be made a finite binary step input at threshold $p+L/N$ with the same logarithmic ratio. For details, approximate the bounded cross from inside by finite axis-grid unions, with support-volume loss tending to zero. Boundary nullity holds for the finite cross away from its coordinate hyperplanes. Each fixed strict witness persists at the weaker threshold for all sufficiently fine steps by $L^1$ approximation. Fatou supplies the required halo lower bound, and domination by the full cross halo at the weaker threshold (r093) supplies the upper bound on the ratio. Thus obtain a bounded finite step packet $D_0$ with true strict halo $H_0$ at $p+L/N$ and $h=|H_0|/|D_0|$ satisfying $\log h=\log Q_N+o_B(L)$. $H_0$ is bounded because a strict side cannot exceed the corresponding bounding-box width divided by its threshold.

Place a sufficiently small dilate of the packet and its halo in one chart of the unit torus, with halo fraction $f\le1/4$ and packet density $f/h$. Take $M=\lceil 5L/f\rceil$ independent uniform translates and let $D$ be the union of the physical packets. For every receiver the expected uncovered fraction (no translated $H_0$) is $(1-f)^M\le N^{-5}$. The probability that the actual uncovered volume exceeds $N^{-2}$ is at most $N^{-3}$. The raw sum of translated source volumes is $a=Mf/h$, and the expected integral of ordered intersections of distinct copies is at most $a^2$. Pointwise the loss from the raw count down to the union indicator is bounded by this ordered intersection count. Markov therefore gives $a/2\le |D|\le a$ except with probability $\le2a=o(1)$. Fix a simultaneous realization: its honest periodic $p+L/N$ halo has volume at least $1-N^{-2}$, it is a finite box-grid step set, and its density $d_N^\circ=|D|$ has the asserted critical price. This construction is done before choosing $m$.

### A general full-volume restriction on extreme side scales

For this step set in $n$ dimensions let $r=\lfloor k/8\rfloor$ and $p_-=1-2\eta$, $p_0=1-3\eta$. There exist $0<b<T<\infty$ and a measurable set $B_{\rm sc}\subset\mathbb T^n$ with
\[
 |B_{\rm sc}|\le C e^{-cN}
\]
such that through $y\notin B_{\rm sc}$, every rectangle having $D$-average $>p_-$ has median length of its first $k$ sides in $[b,T]$. Use either middle convention with at least $\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ coordinates on each appropriate side. The constants $b,T$ may depend on the base grid and on $N$, but not on $m$. We use lengths in the real periodic lift; intervals longer than one turn are also covered by the following argument.

Fix a deterministic set $J\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}$ of $r$ coordinates. For short sides of lengths $<b$, discard a common first-coordinate boundary neighborhood of the finite grid of measure at most $e^{-2N}$ by choosing $b$ sufficiently small (periodic distance). Off this set the coordinates of $J$ are frozen inside their same grid cells. Thus a true rectangle of mean $>p_-$ whose $J$ sides are short gives a true $(n-r)$-dimensional rectangle of mean $>p_0$ for the fixed slice $D_{y_J}$. Integration of the genuine periodic indicator bound over $y_J$ gives the measure upper $H_{n-r}(p_0)d_N^\circ+e^{-2N}$. This is an ordinary family of slices, rather than the diagonal of $r$ duplicated receivers.

For long sides of lengths $>T$, the periodized one-dimensional uniform average has density at most $1+1/T$ relative to uniform on the corresponding circle (each point is covered at most $\lceil |I|\rceil$ times). With these coordinates integrated away, their product average is bounded by $(1+1/T)^r$ times the full $J$ marginal $e_J(y_{J^c})=\int_{\mathbb T^r}1_D(y_J,y_{J^c})dy_J$. Choose $T$ so large that $p_-/(1+1/T)^r>p_0$. A long-side success gives an honest $(n-r)$-dimensional witness on $e_J$ at threshold $p_0$. The inequality for all $0\le e\le1$, $|\{M_S e>p_0\}|\le H_{n-r}(p_0)\int e$, follows by periodic finite-box transference and the zero-cost indicator purification in r092 §1, or by mass-preserving fine grid purification on the torus. Hence the long-side receivers also have measure at most $H_{n-r}(p_0)d_N^\circ$.

In either case r091 (6) gives $\log H_{n-r}(p_0)\le\mu(n-r)+O(L)$ because $1-p_0+2(n-r)^{-4}<1/2$ eventually and the upper Gaussian inverse term is negative. Since $\log d_N^\circ=-\mu N+o(N)$ both estimates are bounded by $\exp(-\mu r+o(N))$. It is essential not to take a union over all $\binom k r$ slices. If a particular offending rectangle has at least $\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ short (respectively long) front sides, a uniformly sampled $r$-subset lies among them with probability at least $(3/8)^r$ for large $k$: each draw without replacement has conditional probability at least $(\lfloor k/2\rfloor-r+1)/k\ge3/8$. Take the measurable supersets from the two preceding paragraphs for each fixed $J$ and define $B_{\rm sc}$ by the event that the fraction of certifying $J$'s in at least one of the two tests is $\ge(3/8)^r$. Integration bounds its measure by $2(8/3)^r[H_{n-r}(p_0)d_N^\circ+e^{-2N}]\le Ce^{-cN}$, because $\mu>\log(8/3)$ and $r\sim N/16$. This gives the stated all-witness restriction on actual receivers, with no measurable choice among the offending rectangles needed. The argument works for any finite step input at this density.

### Encoding disjoint ranges of actual sizes

Partition the last circle into half-open arcs $P_1,\ldots,P_m$ of length $1/m$. Choose positive integers $K_1=1$ and $K_{j+1}>2(T/b)K_j$. On $P_j$ take
\[
  1_{E_m}(y,z)=1_D(K_j y\bmod1).
\]
Uniform integer multiplication preserves Haar measure, so $|E_m|=|D|$ exactly, irrespective of the number or size of the frequencies. In any panel interior, each preimage of a genuine base-successful point has a genuine full-dimensional box witness: scale the base witness back by $K_j$, and take the $z$ side within $P_j$. The base coverage proves (1). The full true periodic score is in $[(1-N^{-2})/d_N^\circ,1/d_N^\circ]$, so its logarithm is $\log Q_N+o_B(L)$ on both sides.

Define the exceptional panel indicator $W(y,z)=1_{B_{\rm sc}}(K_j y)$ for $z\in P_j$. It has total volume at most $Ce^{-cN}$, independently of the number of panels. Discard receivers with $M_z W(y,\cdot)(z)>1/4$. This costs at most $7Ce^{-cN}$ by the sharp one-dimensional periodic indicator estimate. Fix any remaining receiver and any true successful product $R_y\times I_z$ through it. Let $s$ be the median of its first $k$ side lengths. Among all panels there is at most one index $j_*$ for which $s\in[b/K_{j_*},T/K_{j_*}]$, because these intervals are disjoint. For a given panel $j$ the actual $R_y$ average equals the $D$ average on the box $K_jR_y$ through $K_j y$ in the periodic lift. For $j\ne j_*$ this average is at most $p_-$ unless that panel has $W(y,\cdot)=1$. In all cases the average is at most one. Consequently
\[
 p<m_{R_y\times I_z}1_{E_m}\le p_-+(1-p_-)
       \big[m_{I_z}1_{P_{j_*}}+m_{I_z}W(y,\cdot)\big],
\]
where the first indicator is zero if there is no decoded index. Since $(p-p_-)/(1-p_-)=1/2$ and the last term's average is $\le1/4$, the decoded index must exist and $m_{I_z}1_{P_{j_*}}>1/4$. Thus the actual receiver coordinate $z$ lies in the fixed set $C_{j_*}=\{M_11_{P_{j_*}}>1/4\}$ of measure at most $7/m$. The common first-block box used in all rows of the *same* $I_z$ is what enforces this conclusion, even when a chosen witness spans many panels.

For any selector the decoder $j_*$ depends only on $(A_1,\ldots,A_k)$; define an empty decoded set when it has no index. The event $z\in C_{j_*}$ has probability at least $\theta-C'e^{-cN}$ under the true uniform-receiver law. Under the product of the marginal law of $(X_{\le k},A_{\le k})$ and an independent uniform $z$, its probability is at most $7/m$. The conditional information against this reference is $I(A_{\le k};z\mid X_{\le k})$, since the physical receiver's $z$ is originally independent of its first $k$ coordinates. Binary data processing bounds it below by $(\theta-C'e^{-cN})\log(m/7)-\log2$. Finally each summand in the left of (2) is at least $I(A_i;z\mid X_{\le k},A_{<i})$: first expose $X_{i+1:k}$ within $X_{>i}$ via the chain rule, then retain $z$. Summation proves (2).

**Consequence for the r096 dual.** The scale parameter $m$ can tend to infinity with the base region, base density, threshold and base coverage all fixed. Hence there can be no finite upper bound on the minimum necessary lookahead of all macroscopically successful selections depending only on dimension, threshold and source density, even on a near-benchmark sequence. No rigorous inequality of the form ‘a source must pay an unbounded extra density factor as its least required receiver lookahead tends to infinity’ can hold under these hypotheses. In r096 (7), the actual test source is itself feasible for every finite subfamily of its strict-success rectangles, with cost $d_N^\circ$ regardless of $m$. The information growth therefore cannot by itself make their linear-program dual prices exceed $d_N^\circ$. The example uses unbounded geometric frequency range and does not rule out a price for particular scale-restricted coding schemes, a different charge for *productive* lookahead, or the unrestricted upper $H_N(p_N)\le Q_NN^{o(1)}$.

### One source enforces the phenomenon for every fixed coordinate order

One can avoid depending on a designated favorable order. Use $N$ coordinates $(x_1,\ldots,x_{N-1},t)$ with $M_0=N-1$ mode intervals of equal length on the $t$ circle. In mode $j$, use $x_j$ as the panel coordinate in the preceding construction, dividing it into the same $m$ equal panel arcs $P_l$. Put the source value equal to $1_D(K_l x_{-j})$, where $x_{-j}$ means the $N-2$ nondistinguished coordinates among $x_1,\ldots,x_{N-1}$. Use one and the same base finite step $D$ now in $n=N-2$ dimensions at the same near-benchmark density, chosen at $p+L/N$ as above. In each pair of mode/panel interiors keep the $t$ and $x_j$ sides inside those intervals. Thus the exact source density is still $|D|$, independent of $m$, and the true coverage is at least $1-N^{-2}$.

Fix *any* permutation of these $N$ receiver coordinates. Set $h=\lfloor (N-1)/128\rfloor$, take its first $h$ coordinates, and let $S$ be the subset of their indices other than $t$. Its size $k$ equals $h$ or $h-1$. The same $b,T$ can be chosen in advance for all orders: include the finite grid-face neighborhoods in every base coordinate in choosing $b$, and take $T$ sufficient for the largest of the two possible $r=\lfloor k/8\rfloor$. The median lemma applies to any specified $k$ base coordinates, since it used only the number $k$, the full grid and the unrestricted lower-dimensional weak bound. Its exponential error remains $Ce^{-cN}$, with a smaller absolute $c>0$, since $r/N$ still has a fixed positive limit. For each $j\notin S$ it therefore applies in mode $j$ with the same set $S$ of *physical* measured sides. The exceptional set for this estimate may depend on the order and the mode; one common receiver exceptional set over all permutations is not needed.

Let $s$ be the median length of the $S$ sides in a candidate honest box. As before $s$ selects at most one frequency index $l_*$, and that index is common to **all** modes $j\notin S$. For each such mode transfer its exceptional set into a panel indicator $W_j(x)$ by testing $K_{l(x_j)}x_{-j}$ in the base exceptional set for the appropriate coordinates. The total measure of $W_j$ is at most $Ce^{-cN}$. Off a set of measure $\le15M_0Ce^{-cN}$ every $M_{x_j}W_j\le1/8$ simultaneously for all these $j$. This union is over at most $N-1$ coordinate modes, not the arbitrarily many frequency panels. In the $t$ coordinate let $U$ be the union of modes with $j\in S$; $|U|\le1/128$. The actual one-dimensional halo $\{M_t1_U>1/8\}$ has measure at most $15/128$. Discard it as well.

Write $a_j$ for the fraction of the true $t$-side in mode $j$. The discarded $t$ set guarantees $\sum_{j\in S}a_j\le1/8$ on every remaining receiver and side. The modes in $S$ may receive the trivial density upper one. For each mode $j\notin S$ the preceding common-box calculation gives the upper $p_-+(1-p_-)[m_{I_j}1_{P_{l_*}}+1/8]$ for its whole $x$-block mean, with zero indicator if no index is decoded. Averaging with the actual $a_j$, strict success implies
\[
 \tfrac12<\sum_{j\in S}a_j+\sum_{j\notin S}a_j\,m_{I_j}1_{P_{l_*}}+\tfrac18 .
\]
Consequently some $j\notin S$ satisfies $m_{I_j}1_{P_{l_*}}>1/4$, so its still-unobserved receiver coordinate lies in $C_{l_*}$ of measure at most $7/m$. Given the first $h$ sides, the decoder need not know which mode was responsible. Its test simply asks if *some* unopened nondistinguished coordinate belongs to $C_{l_*}$. If future receiver coordinates are independent of the prefix and its sides, this event has probability at most $7(N-1)/m$ by a union bound. Under any actual selection serving a fraction/probability $\theta$ it has probability at least $\theta-15/128-15(N-1)Ce^{-cN}$. Thus for the *same* finite source, for every fixed permutation, and every physical selection in that order, the first $h$ information terms satisfy, for $m>7(N-1)$,
\[
 \mathcal I_{\rm first\ h}^{\rm(order)}
 \ \ge [\theta-15/128-C'e^{-c'N}]\log\frac{m}{7(N-1)}-\log 2 .          \tag{3}
\]
This follows by the same conditional-KL chain rule, retaining the set of all unopened receiver coordinates this time. At $\theta\ge1/2$ the coefficient is positive uniformly. Hence even an order chosen optimally *in advance* cannot bound the necessary information by a function of the density and dimension alone. An adaptive order chosen from already read coordinates is a different problem: in particular reading $t$ first reveals the mode. No claim for such an order is made.

**A scale-free decoder.** In the all-order construction, even discarding the overall size of the selected prefix sides does not restore a density bound on the information. In panel $l$ of mode $j$, replace the integer multiplication on each other axis $a$ by the axis-specific integer frequency $K_l^a$ (use the original axis indices $a\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}$ in every mode). This preserves both density and available local true witnesses by the corresponding positive diagonal dilation. For a fixed order as above, split its physical index set $S$ into a lower and upper half according to index, $S_{\rm lo},S_{\rm hi}$, of sizes differing by at most one. Use the base median restriction for *both* subsets in each late mode; the dimension-drop proof works since each subgroup has cardinality a fixed positive fraction of $N$, and a union over two exceptional sets has the same exponential bound. One common $b,T$ works independently of the order and mode as before.

Write $s_g$ for the median of the **original** physical side lengths in subgroup $g$, before scaling. In a panel on which the two scaled medians lie in $[b,T]$, monotonicity of the median in each entry gives
\[
 b/K_l^{\max g}\le s_g\le T/K_l^{\min g},\qquad g\in\{S_{\rm lo},S_{\rm hi}\}.
\]
Since $\min S_{\rm hi}-\max S_{\rm lo}\ge1$, it follows that
\[
       \frac{b}{T K_l^N}\le \frac{s_{\rm hi}}{s_{\rm lo}}
                \le\frac{T}{b K_l} .                                   \tag{4}
\]
Choose $K_{l+1}>(T/b)^2 K_l^N$. These possible ratio intervals are disjoint for all labels, independently of $S$. A nonmatching panel therefore necessarily violates the median bound in at least one of its two groups. The previous $W_j$ construction uses the union of their base exceptional sets, and every other step giving (3) is unchanged. More specifically, conditional on the observed receiver prefix, the **single dimensionless ratio** in (4) itself has mutual information about the future receiver coordinates bounded below by the right side of (3): it is the only statistic of the prefix sides used in the decoder and the independent-reference event. It is invariant under a common isotropic resizing of those sides. This refined example still requires an unbounded range of physical aspect ratios and gives no universal conclusion under a fixed bound on that range.

**Local scale check.** Local single-core Python checked $\binom{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}{r}/\binom{k}{r}\ge(3/8)^r$ by exact rational arithmetic at $N=257,1025,4097$ for the first construction. The logarithmic selection penalty per frozen coordinate was $0.76002482$, $0.76361774$, $0.76451972$, respectively; the analytic coarse margin uses $\mu-\log(8/3)=0.29763529\ldots$. The ratio $(p-p_-)/(1-p_-)=1/2$ was also checked exactly at $\eta=1/40,1/1000$. No finite numerical onset for the high-dimensional density theorem follows from these checks.

## 2. Ancillary block-causal entropy upper (independent of the counterexample)

### Allowing full lookahead within blocks of up to fifteen coordinates

The exponential obstruction persists for a strictly bigger class. Split the receiver coordinates into blocks of sizes $1\le b_j\le15$. A causal block policy can see the **entire current block** before selecting its genuine block rectangle, with no restriction on the coordinate choices within it. It may use earlier blocks, sides, and earlier sampled values. A uniform product receiver still has independent uniform fresh blocks given the entire earlier history. For $p_N$ and $\theta_N\to1$, a policy giving $P(Y\in E)\ge p_N\theta_N$ must satisfy
\[
 \log(1/d)\le [2\log 2-\tfrac1{30}\log(1+15 e^2/4)+o(1)]N
             \le (1.275+o(1))N < (\mu-o(1))N.                          \tag{4c}
\]
The same conclusion for a true simultaneous physical selection satisfying the block-causality restriction is an immediate specialization.

Here is the block envelope. In the r011 rank comparison of a block indicator, any predictable hit sum is stochastically dominated by a sum $S_b$ of $b$ independent positive variables $L_i$ with $\Pr(L_i>t)=2/(1+e^t)$ for $t\ge0$. Indeed for every possible action $r$ the single-step upper hit probability is bounded by this quantity (the r011 one-step optimum is attained at $r=e^{-t}$); sequential conditional quantile coupling to independent uniforms gives domination for arbitrary predictable choices. Thus $H_b(t)\le\exp[s_t(S_b)]$ for $0<t<1$ by r011's physical indicator-to-rank comparison. This one-sided comparison is used only for the upper. For a uniform $b$-dimensional receiver and one chosen physical box the mass it sends to any set of size $u$ is at most $\int_0^1\min(1,u H_b(t))\,dt$, by primitive level integration and torus transference, as in (5). Its upper bound is precisely $G_b(u)=\Pr(T_b>-\log u)$ where $T_b=S_b+Z$, $Z$ is an independent unit exponential: condition on the quantile of $S_b$ indexed by uniform $t$. The derivative $g_b(u)=e^s f_{T_b}(s)$, $s=-\log u$, is decreasing in $u$ since $e^s f_{T_b}(s)=\int_0^s e^v f_{S_b}(v)dv$. It integrates to one. In fact $g_b(e^{-s})\le 2^b s^b/b!$ by convolution of the $L_i$ densities bounded by $2e^{-t}$. All required positive moments are finite.

For any fresh block, its conditional output density is dominated in the positive-part inequalities by $g_b$. Its relative entropy is therefore at most $D_b=\int g_b\log g_b=\mathbb ET_b-h(T_b)$, where $h$ is ordinary differential entropy; use the change of variable $u=e^{-s}$. The half-logistic law has $\mathbb E L_i=2\log2$ by tail integration and $h(L_i)=2-\log2$: if $U=\tanh(L_i/2)$ is uniform on $(0,1)$, its density at $L_i$ is $(1-U^2)/2$. Integrate its negative logarithm to obtain the entropy. As $h(Z)=1$, entropy power addition gives
\[
 h(T_b)\ge1+\tfrac12\log(1+b e^2/4),\qquad
 D_b\le 2b\log2-\tfrac12\log(1+b e^2/4).
\]
For clarity the needed one-dimensional entropy power inequality follows here by Gaussian smoothing. For independent $U,V$ of the types above and $0<a<1$, write $U_t=U+\sqrt t G_1$, $V_t=V+\sqrt t G_2$ with independent standard normals. The difference $h(\sqrt a\,U_t+\sqrt{1-a}\,V_t)-a h(U_t)-(1-a)h(V_t)$ is nonincreasing in $t$: heat differentiation and integration by parts give one half the analogous difference of Fisher informations; the score of the sum is the conditional expectation of $\sqrt a$ times the score of $U_t$ plus $\sqrt{1-a}$ times the score of $V_t$, so Jensen and the zero mean scores give nonpositivity. The entropy difference tends to zero as $t\to\infty$ by the conditional Gaussian lower bound and matched-variance Gaussian upper bound. At zero the needed entropies converge by dominated convergence: the densities have uniformly bounded exponential upper tails under sufficiently small Gaussian smoothing, and $|x\log x|\le C\sqrt x$ on a bounded density range. Positive-time heat identities follow from Gaussian convolution, with the boundary terms vanishing by the exponential tails (or by smooth cutoffs and the finite Fisher bound $1/t$). Rescaling $U,V$ by $1/\sqrt a,1/\sqrt{1-a}$ respectively and optimizing $a$ gives $e^{2h(U+V)}\ge e^{2h(U)}+e^{2h(V)}$. Iteration yields the displayed estimate for $T_b$. Already for $b=2$ the simpler conditioning bound $h(T_2)\ge h(L_1)$ alone gives $D_2/2\le1.233<\mu$.

The bound on $D_b/b$ is increasing in $b$, hence at most $2\log2-\log(1+15e^2/4)/30=1.274387385\ldots<\mu$ for all allowed blocks. The entropy chain rule gives $D(Q_Y\|\mathrm{Unif})\le\sum_jD_{b_j}$. Binary data processing then gives $(p\theta)\log(1/d)-\log2\le\sum_jD_{b_j}$ for the macroscopic success event, proving (4c). This uses genuine receiver blocks with arbitrary within-block rectangles at every step; it does not assert the bound for a selector that also reads unopened receiver blocks. As with one coordinate, the power moments of $g_b$ can instead be used for a change-of-measure estimate when future receiver information is available. No optimality of the block size fifteen is claimed.
