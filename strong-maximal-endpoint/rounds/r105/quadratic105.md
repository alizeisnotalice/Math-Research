# Keeping the signed curvature in the prefix comparison

Use the section class of r100, $L=\log N$, $n=N-k$, $\rho=\sqrt{L/N}$, and fix $B$. We propose the sharp range $k=o(N^{2/3})$. The whole-space rearrangement comparison is r103 §3. For clarity calibrate at the **suffix** threshold $p_n=1-\tau_n-B\log(n)/n$, and put $s_0=s_{p_n}^{\rm det}$, $f_0=\phi(z_n)/(\sigma\sqrt n)$, $s=z_n/(\sigma\sqrt n)$. The desired full rank is $a=|E|\exp(\mu N-d_N+(c_Q+\epsilon)L)$.

Here is the signed local estimate for $g=M_n1_{E_z}$ as a function on the whole ambient product. For $|u|\le3W$, $W=A(\sqrt{kL}+L)$, one has
\[
 g^*(|E|e^{s_0+u})\le p_n-f_0\{u+s u^2/2-C[1+\rho L|u|+(L^2/n)u^2+\rho^2|u|^3]\}.             \tag{1}
\]
Indeed $\rho W=o(1)$ in this range. Use r103's uniform version of r094 (15) at the single tested rank, with its one matched iid action $c=\mu+O(\rho)$ held fixed for *both* CDF evaluations. At $s_0$ the iid CDF is at least $1-p_n$. Use the one-term iid Edgeworth CDF at both endpoints, with absolute errors $O(1/n)$. Its Gaussian-plus-polynomial main term has first derivative at $s_0$ equal to $f_0[1+O(\rho L)]$, second derivative $f_0[s+O(L^2/n)]$, and third derivative $O(f_0\rho^2)$ throughout the interval. For these derivative bounds its variance is $\mu n+O(\sqrt{nL})$, its mean is $\mu n+O(L)$, its standardized argument at the center is $-z_n+O(L/\sqrt n)$ and the standardized third cumulant is $O(1/\sqrt n)$. The Edgeworth correction to the first derivative is the Gaussian density times a cubic Hermite polynomial times $O(n^{-1/2})$; further derivatives differentiate this explicit polynomial and Gaussian. The density stays within a constant factor across the window since $\rho W=o(1)$. Thus Taylor with remainder gives the above lower CDF difference after division by $f_0$. The relative comparison loss $O(n^{-1/2})$ costs $O(1)$ on rank scale as the matched CDF is $O(\tau_n)$. Supporting halfspaces followed by the true sectional volume integral give (1), as in r103.

For an arbitrary predictable prefix policy set $D=\sum[\mu-E_{i-1}Z_i]$, $M=\sum(Z_i-E_{i-1}Z_i)$ and $D'=\min(D,W)$. Write $x_0=\log(a/|E|)-\mu k-s_0=d_n-d_N+\epsilon L+o(L)$, $x=x_0-M$. Then the actual rank offset equals $x+D$. Monotonicity permits $D'$ in its place. The uniform conditional exponential moment bound yields $E|M|^j=O_j(k^{j/2}+1)$ for $j\le 6$, and $P(|x|>W)\le N^{-10}$ for large fixed $A$, since $|x_0|=O(\rho k+L)$. On the central event the signed bracket at $x+D'$ in (1) is bounded below by $x+sx^2/2+D'/2$ minus $C[1+\rho L|x|+(L^2/n)x^2+\rho^2|x|^3]$. This follows by expanding the square, $sW=o(1)$, and absorbing the powers of $D'$ in the error using $D'\le W$, $\rho^2W^2=o(1)$. The discarded event contributes $o(1)$ on the rank scale by the moments above and $g^*\le1$. The expected absolute error is $o(L)$: its cubic part is $O((L/N)(k^{3/2}+L^3+1))$, and the other parts are smaller than $L$. We used $\rho k=O(\sqrt k)$ here. Hence the bracket in expectation is at least $x_0+s E M^2/2+E D'/2-o(L)$.

The coordinate moment stability of r022 gives $|\operatorname{Var}_{i-1}(Z_i)-\mu|\le C\sqrt{\mu-E_{i-1}Z_i}$. Therefore $s|E M^2-\mu k|\le C E[s\sqrt{kD}]$. On $D\le W$ Young absorbs this in an arbitrarily small fixed multiple of $D'$ plus $O(s^2 k)=o(L)$. On $D>W$ use $D\le Ck$ and $sk=o(W)$, so this is also absorbed by $D'$. We obtain $E g^*(ae^{-S_k})\le p_n-f_0[x_0+\mu sk/2-o(L)]$.

Finally the physical thresholds must be transported at the same accuracy. One has
\[
 d_N-d_n=\mu sk/2+(\tau_n-\tau_N)/f_0+o(L).
\]
Indeed expand $\sigma(\sqrt N-\sqrt n)z_N$ to first order, with error $O(k^2\sqrt L/n^{3/2})$, and use $\tau_n-\tau_N=\phi(z_n)(z_N-z_n)[1+O(k/n)]$, since $z_N-z_n=O(k/(n\sqrt L))$. The $B$-terms in $(p_N-p_n)/f_0$ differ from $(\tau_n-\tau_N)/f_0$ by $O_B(k/n)$. Substitution gives $E g^*(ae^{-S_k})\le p_N-f_0[\epsilon L-o(L)]$. Strict physical success at rank $a$ is impossible. Letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ and taking the full-dimensional equal-weight cross as lower example gives the claimed sharp coefficient. The proof uses conditional variance matched to drift and the sign of the suffix rank curvature. The absolute third-order remainder limits this argument to $k=o(N^{2/3})$.
