# R044: general witness-payment redistribution diagnostics

This batch examines 76 cached R038 final payment vectors, excluding the three with separately audited R038 mass-LP certificates. It does not establish a new C_n bound, nor does it rerun the old baseline selection, atoms, or mass LP. The cached accepted-pair sequence is replayed solely to reconstruct the hash-checked final q, h, U and complete cliques.

For each case one full witness-mass LP is proposed numerically and its primal and dual vectors are rationalized and repaired. Exact Fraction checks verify every covering and packing constraint. The dual produces a legal explicit global payment q with exact Σh_iq_i=U and full-clique load no larger than U/M_lower. Independent stdlib checking (`verify_exact.py`) passes all 76 saved primal/dual certificates, initial q hashes, payment sums and full-clique loads; 67 saved global candidates strictly improve the starting load. These feasible redistributions concern a fixed payment-certificate family, not operator lower bounds or proofs that the numerical LP solution is exact optimum.

The first 32 three-coordinate proposal calls per case initially all returned solver status 4 because the first full LP initialized the HiGHS global scheduler with its default thread setting and the following calls requested threads=1. Those failures are retained as implementation diagnostics, not mathematical infeasibility. A separately registered retry used fresh worker processes and initialized HiGHS uniformly with threads=1, retried only the same 2432 triples, and did not rerun any full mass LP. It produced 2418 exact-feasible rational candidates; 14 rational reconstructions had a negative anchor and were rejected. Eight of the 76 states have a strictly smaller exact load among these 32 proposals. The initial full-LP run used single-thread environment variables but did not explicitly fix HiGHS's global thread option; its actual internal thread count was not established.

R044-B selected the 15 largest relative gaps to an explicit feasible global payment among R038 nonedge-coordinate stops. It skipped the previous 32 triples and inspected at most 512 new triples per state, stopping on the first exact strict load reduction. Its 8 membership-class LP proposes load, sum-q and energy stages numerically; the saved rational candidate is checked exactly. These numerical stages are **not an exact proof of lexicographic or triple optimality**. The batch inspected 6702 new triples: 13 states reached 512 without a floating strict-load proposal, and two produced exact strict-load improvements:

- C/1/13 full: coordinates [1,3,9], improvement about 16.6666666657% of initial load.
- C/4/7 full: coordinates [1,13,16], improvement about 16.6666666657% of initial load.

Both start from R038 nonedge-coordinate stops; they were not established as R039 all-pair stops. The other 13 results do not prove triple impossibility. `verify_exact.py` independently checks the saved B candidates by exact payment, nonnegativity and every complete-clique load.

R044-C then tested the 13 B no-result states and two B-success controls, followed by the remaining 61 states because an initial task finished within the registered five-second queue trigger. For every state with an explicit feasible global candidate of strictly smaller load, the MILP proposes a minimum changed-coordinate support to reach at least half that feasible gap. Every coordinate is bounded by the target load because it belongs to a maximal clique; this provides a valid finite big-M. Each solve was limited to 50000 branch nodes, zero requested relative MIP gap and no wall limit; HiGHS was initialized with threads=1 within each fresh worker. Of 76 states, nine lacked a certified feasible global gap and were skipped, while 67 returned numerical status 0. Rational reconstruction and exact full-clique/payment verification yielded 38 feasible vectors; 29 numerical solutions did not yield a verified exact vector. These 29 are not proofs of infeasibility. Five exact vectors change exactly three coordinates; 17 change four, ten change five, two change six and four change 8–12. A numerical MILP optimality status is not a proof of minimum support. `verify_C_exact.py` independently passes all 38 saved rational candidates and the 76 case identities; no integer optimality claim is made.

Source, input hashes, finite budgets and actual timestamps are in `registration.json`, `triple_retry_registration.json`, `B_registration.json` and `C_registration.json`. Per-case geometry, complete cliques, q, h, mass-LP primal/dual, numerical statuses, proposed triples, exact candidates, PID, CPU/wall and RSS remain in the local JSONs. No full source results were deleted.

Resource accounting: the initial full-LP batch had 15 distinct worker PIDs, 2.91 s wall and 10.04 s total worker CPU; its first monitor sample occurred before workers started and no later 5-second sample was possible. The triple retry had 15 distinct workers, 1.75 s wall and 5.98 s summed CPU, again too short for a useful five-second sample. R044-B's 0.547 s startup sample observed 15 workers, 1096.6% aggregate CPU (about 10.97 cores) and about 1.10 GiB combined RSS; this is a sample, not a claim of sustained full utilization. R044-C finished in about 0.491 s, before its 0.5-second sampling interval; its monitor file is empty and no system-wide utilization claim is made. All batches ended on their registered counts or first-improvement conditions; no load was added for utilization alone.
