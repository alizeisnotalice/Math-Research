# R004 paired cutoff diagnostic

The fifteen registered tasks completed, each drawing 2²⁰ Gamma(1/2,1) 64-vectors and normalizing to a Dirichlet shape. The four epsilons were evaluated on the same shapes within each task. Total: 15,728,640 shapes, 62,914,560 cutoff terms and 47,185,920 adjacent time-band differences. Three points each have five independent PCG64 streams; streams are distinct between points, so comparisons between points are not paired. Fixed stream identities and precise points are in registration.md and points.json. The numerical near point is the binary64 conversion of the recorded rational coordinate, not exact rational arithmetic during kernel evaluation.

Point 0 is (v55+v63)/2, hence rank two and in S3. Point 1 is the registered 1/1000003 perturbation of that point; point 2 is the R003 1/1024 sampled-minimum Sobol point. Both off-S3 points have seven different absolute coordinate values, whereas a three-body span has at most four, giving an exact exclusion. The seven hexadecimal coordinates and exact rational formula are retained. G is infinite at point 0; Hε is finite for every ε>0.

The seed spread and the dominance of rare shapes are central. At ε=1/16384, contributions from individual terms at least 0.01 represented about 99.95%, 99.997%, and 9.2% of the three point totals, respectively. For U=P55+P63, shapes with U<0.001 contributed about 99.976%, 99.998%, and 48.3%. These are observed fractions of this sample, not a tail bound. The first two points show extremely unstable rare short-time contributions; the nearby off-S3 result neither implies divergence nor estimates its true small-ε limit reliably. The off-S3 Sobol result appears much more stable under these particular cutoffs and samples, but is not a certificate. Full fixed-bin counts/sums, paired band differences, sample variances, 16 within-stream block means and seed means are in the JSON files. Standard errors or block agreement do not certify deterministic expectation error or global face coverage; these results establish no g or C7 bound.

The recorded main-run wall time was 10.783 s and the sum of worker task CPU times 80.429 s, about 7.46 core equivalents averaged across wall time. Fifteen workers competed with another 15-worker batch; two observed snapshots of this batch reported 797% and 826% summed process CPU and 1,062,896 and 1,065,008 KiB summed RSS, respectively. The latter sums process RSS and can double-count shared pages; it is not a system memory peak. Swap used was 766.81 MiB in the three samples. These are observed snapshots rather than full-time occupancy; process peak RSS individually was 77,529,088 bytes. The resource log also includes other process rows: the figures above filter parent PID 69899. No memory was allocated merely to fill available RAM.

285 ill-conditioned shapes were recomputed individually at 80 decimal digits using the identical original binary64 Gamma draws; no shape was discarded or redrawn. Remaining calculations used binary64 Cholesky on a scaled SPD matrix, with nonfinite or negative quantities treated as failures, and complementary beta evaluations to reduce cancellation in small time bands. Fallback logs preserve each stream/chunk/offset and the SHA-256 of its original Gamma draw, with deterministic seed and ordered stream replay available. These finite floating computations are numerical evidence, not interval arithmetic. The original cache was not opened or changed, and no full-G optimizer ran.

The evidence supports a deterministic positive-mass/interval integration strategy that explicitly handles small U and short-time bands. It also shows that picking a cutoff solely from ordinary seed agreement is unsafe. A proof requires interval shape-mass/variation control and spatial coverage, not the finite sample or its variance.
